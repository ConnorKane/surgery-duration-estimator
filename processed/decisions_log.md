## Stage 2 — Label definition

### Which timestamps define the duration label
- **Choice:** SURGICAL time: `duration_minutes = (opend - opstart) / 60`.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) Anesthesia time `aneend - anestart`; (b) EMR-provided `opdur`/`anedur`; (c) `caseend - casestart` recording length.
- **Why:** Three reasons. (1) **Consistency with the leakage boundary:** the task fixes the feature cutoff at `opstart`, so the natural quantity to predict is the time that elapses AFTER `opstart` — i.e. operation time. Anesthesia time starts at `anestart` (< opstart), which would put the label's clock partly inside the feature window and muddy the leakage contract. (2) **Data quality:** operation time is clean here (min 1.4, max 955 min, zero non-positive, zero >24h, zero nulls), whereas anesthesia time has corrupt rows (a negative -61.5M-min value and a >24h value). (3) **Semantics:** the project is 'surgery duration prediction'; operation start→end is the surgical procedure itself. Anesthesia time (incl. induction/emergence) is more OR-occupancy than 'surgery' and is the better *alternative* to revisit for a room-turnover model — noted for v2.

### Impossible-label filter
- **Choice:** Drop rows where duration is null, <= 0, or > 1440 min (24h).
- **Alternatives considered:** Keep and cap extreme durations; or use a tighter cap (e.g. 12h).
- **Why:** Non-positive/null durations are corrupt timestamps and cannot be a valid target. 24h is a generous physical ceiling for a single OR case; anything beyond it is a timestamp error rather than a real surgery. A tighter cap (12h) risks discarding genuine long cases (this cohort has a real 955-min case), so we use the conservative 24h ceiling here and handle the upper TAIL via winsorized features later — never by dropping real long surgeries.

- Rows before label filter: 6388
- Dropped null duration: 0
- Dropped non-positive duration: 0
- Dropped >24h duration: 0
- Rows after label filter: 6388

## Stage 3 — Cohort filtering

Every exclusion rule is applied in sequence; the table below logs the rows dropped by each rule (rules are evaluated on the surviving cohort, so counts are sequential, not independent).

### Anesthesia-type cohort
- **Choice:** Keep `General` and `Spinal`; exclude `Sedationalgesia` (MAC/sedation).
- **Alternatives considered:** Keep only `General`; or keep all three.
- **Why:** Sedation/MAC ('Sedationalgesia') is the local-anesthesia-care analog the brief asks us to exclude — different workflow, poorly-defined operative phase. `Spinal` is genuine surgical (neuraxial/regional) anesthesia with a well-defined operation and is kept. Keeping only `General` would needlessly drop 273 legitimate regional cases.

### ASA physical-status scope
- **Choice:** Exclude ASA == 6 (brain-dead organ donors).
- **Alternatives considered:** Keep all ASA classes; or also exclude ASA 5 (moribund) — none present here.
- **Why:** ASA 6 are organ-procurement cases, not scheduled therapeutic surgery; their duration distribution is driven by harvest logistics, not the patient/procedure features we model. Excluding them keeps the target population coherent. ASA nulls are NOT dropped here — ASA is imputed with a flag in Stage 4 to avoid discarding otherwise-complete cases.

### Incomplete-record filter
- **Choice:** Require non-null `age` and `sex`.
- **Alternatives considered:** Also require non-null height/weight/BMI and all labs.
- **Why:** Age and sex are the minimal, near-universal patient descriptors and are cheap to require. We deliberately do NOT require height/weight/BMI, ASA, or labs to be present — those are imputed with missingness flags (Stage 4) so we retain cases that are otherwise informative. Dropping on every field would shrink the cohort and bias it toward fully-documented (often sicker/work-up-heavy) patients.

- (No separate drop for height/weight errors — implausible values are capped and BMI is imputed in Stage 4 rather than dropped.)

## Stage 4 — Feature engineering (canonical schema)

**Leakage discipline:** a feature may use only information knowable BEFORE `opstart`. Preop labs, comorbidities, patient demographics, the *scheduled* procedure descriptors, and the *planned* start clock are allowed. Anything measured/occurring at or after `opstart` (postop labs, intraop drugs/fluids, EBL, outcomes, the duration itself) is FORBIDDEN and never read into features.

### Age de-identification top-coding
- **Choice:** Map the de-identified string age '>89' -> 90 (8 cases), keep age numeric.
- **Alternatives considered:** Drop >89 cases; or map to 89 / 95 / a separate 'elderly' flag.
- **Why:** Ages 90+ are top-coded to '>89' for anonymity. Mapping to 90 (the band floor) preserves the case and its monotonic age signal with minimal distortion; the exact value in a 90+ band matters little for duration and these are few. Dropping them would bias the cohort younger.

### BMI source
- **Choice:** Recompute `bmi = weight / (height/100)^2`; fall back to provided `bmi` column when height or weight is missing; then cap to [10, 70].
- **Alternatives considered:** Trust the provided `bmi` column outright; or drop rows with bad BMI.
- **Why:** Recomputing from height/weight is transparent and lets us sanity-check the provided column. The [10, 70] cap removes physically impossible BMIs (data-entry errors) without discarding the case — the patient's other features are still useful. Values outside the range are clipped to the bound, and truly-missing BMI is median-imputed with a flag (below).

### Preop-lab join: window and aggregation
- **Choice:** For each case and each key analyte, take the MOST RECENT lab with `dt < opstart` AND `dt >= opstart - 30 days`. 30-day window; strictly before opstart.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) Use clinical_data's preop_* columns directly; (b) 7-day window; (c) 90-day window; (d) mean/min/max over the window instead of most-recent.
- **Why:** We build labs from lab_data (not the convenient preop_* columns) because the brief asks for an explicit, auditable time-windowed join that future data sources can reproduce. **Strictly `dt < opstart`** enforces leakage safety (a lab drawn exactly at/after incision is excluded). **30 days** matches standard preoperative work-up validity: routine preop labs are drawn within days-to-weeks of surgery; 7 days is too tight (misses clinic labs from the prior visit and inflates missingness), 90 days risks stale physiology. **Most-recent** (vs mean) reflects the value the anesthesiologist actually sees at scheduling and avoids blending stale with fresh draws.

### Time-of-day / day-of-week features
- **Choice:** Add `op_hour` and `op_dow` to the schema but leave them NULL in this release.
- **Alternatives considered:** Fabricate them from the relative `opstart` seconds; or omit the columns.
- **Why:** `casestart` is anonymized to 0 for every case, so `opstart` is a seconds-offset into the recording, NOT a wall-clock time — real hour-of-day and day-of-week are unrecoverable. Fabricating them would inject noise/false signal. We keep the columns as typed nullable placeholders so a future, non-anonymized data source (which the brief anticipates) plugs in without a schema change — the same pattern used for staff features below. Note: the *planned* start clock IS a legitimate pre-opstart feature in principle; it is simply not present in this dataset.

### Staff features
- **Choice:** Add nullable `surgeon_id` and `surgeon_case_count_for_this_procedure` columns, both NULL in this release.
- **Alternatives considered:** Omit them until a data source exists.
- **Why:** VitalDB is anonymized with no surgeon identifiers. The brief explicitly wants these columns present so future data (with surgeon IDs) plugs in cleanly. `surgeon_case_count_for_this_procedure` is a surgeon×procedure experience proxy that will need a leakage-safe, time-ordered count when real data arrives.

## Stage 5 — Train/Val/Test split

### Split strategy and ordering key
- **Choice:** Chronological 70/15/15 split. Ordering key = `caseid` ascending (earliest 70% -> train, next 15% -> val, latest 15% -> test).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) Random split; (b) grouped split by subjectid; (c) refuse to split for lack of a timestamp.
- **Why:** A scheduling model is deployed forward in time, so the split MUST be temporal to give an honest generalization estimate and avoid train/test leakage of era effects. BUT this release has no wall-clock timestamp (`casestart`==0). `caseid` (1..6388) is the only monotonic case ordering available and is used as the temporal proxy: test = highest caseids. **Caveat (logged loudly):** the data dictionary describes caseid as a random number, so this proxy may not be truly chronological — when real timestamps arrive, swap the ordering key here and the rest of the pipeline is unchanged. This is strictly better than a random split (never mixes a 'future' case into train) and keeps the mechanism correct. Encoders/scalers/imputers are fit on TRAIN ONLY to prevent leakage across the boundary.

- Total cohort: 6303
- Train: 4412 (caseid 1..4469)
- Val:   945 (caseid 4470..5426)
- Test:  946 (caseid 5427..6388)

## Stage 4b — Imputation, outlier caps, encoding (fit on TRAIN only)

### Numeric outlier handling
- **Choice:** Winsorize labs + age + bmi at TRAIN 0.5th/99.5th percentiles (clip, don't drop). BMI additionally hard-capped to [10,70] earlier.
- **Alternatives considered:** Drop outlier rows; z-score trimming; or no capping.
- **Why:** Clipping preserves every case (rows are scarce and each carries a valid label) while taming data-entry spikes that would distort scalers and any distance/linear component of the downstream model. 0.5/99.5 is gentle — it touches only the extreme tails. Percentiles are computed on TRAIN ONLY and then applied to val/test, so no test information leaks into the caps.

### Missing-value strategy for numeric features
- **Choice:** Median-impute (TRAIN median) every numeric feature that has gaps AND add a companion `<col>_missing` 0/1 indicator column.
- **Alternatives considered:** Drop rows with any missing; model-native NaN handling; mean imputation.
- **Why:** Median is robust to skew (labs, BMI are skewed). Missingness in preop labs is itself informative (a lab not ordered often signals a low-acuity case), so we preserve that signal explicitly with indicator columns rather than letting imputation erase it. Row-dropping would gut lab coverage; we keep the table model-ready (no NaNs in the numeric block) while retaining the signal. Train-median keeps the fit leakage-safe.

### Categorical encoding
- **Choice:** Integer/ordinal label-encoding fit on TRAIN. `opname` (241 levels) is rare-grouped: levels seen <10x in train collapse to 'Other'. Unseen val/test levels and nulls map to a reserved code (-1). Mappings saved to encoders.pkl.
- **Alternatives considered:** One-hot encoding; target/mean encoding; leave as raw strings.
- **Why:** The downstream CDF model will almost certainly be gradient-boosted trees (native support for integer-coded categoricals), so compact label codes are ideal and avoid one-hot blow-up from 241 opnames. Rare-grouping controls cardinality and overfitting on singleton procedures. Target encoding was rejected for v1 because it needs careful out-of-fold handling to avoid label leakage — deferred to the modeling stage. Reserved -1 for unseen/null keeps inference robust to new categories.

### Feature scaling
- **Choice:** Fit a `StandardScaler` on the TRAIN numeric block and SAVE it to encoders.pkl, but store UNSCALED values in features.parquet.
- **Alternatives considered:** Pre-scale the parquet; or skip scaling entirely.
- **Why:** Tree models don't need scaling, but a linear/NN CDF head or quantile regressor might. Saving the fitted scaler (train-only) lets the modeler apply it deterministically if needed, while keeping the persisted table human-readable and model-agnostic. Storing unscaled values avoids baking one model's assumptions into the shared artifact.
